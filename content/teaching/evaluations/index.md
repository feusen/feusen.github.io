I have taught or assisted in 19 courses at UC Santa Cruz since 2022: one as instructor of record and 18 as a teaching assistant or grader. Sixteen assistant courses and the instructor course have student evaluations (SETs).[^missing] Every figure below is the share of ratings in the top two categories ("Frequently" or "Very Frequently") on UCSC's six-point scale, excluding "Unable to comment." I am currently instructor of record for Intermediate Macroeconomics at San José State University (Fall 2026); evaluations for that course are not yet available.

[^missing]: Two courses have no evaluations: Public Finance (Econ 150), where I was not the reporting TA, and Mathematical Methods (Econ 210B), the PhD summer math course, which received no responses.

## Instructor of record: Intermediate Macroeconomics (Econ 100B), Summer 2025

A required upper-division course taught as a compressed, synchronous online summer session. Fifteen of 50 students (30%) rated the instructor. Students rated lectures, class activities, and grading communication in the top two categories unanimously. The lowest item, opportunities to interact with other students, reflects the online format.

![Instructor of record, Econ 100B (Summer 2025): percent of ratings Frequently or Very Frequently, by survey item, with respondent counts.](/teaching/fig_ior.webp)

## Teaching assistant: sixteen courses

Across all assistant evaluations, 92% of ratings on clarity and organization, 90% on engagement and participation, and 92% on support and accessibility fall in the top two categories (figure and table below). The strongest results come from the largest sections: Introduction to Econometrics in Winter 2026 (29 respondents) and Intermediate Macroeconomics in Spring 2025 (22 respondents).

![Teaching assistant evaluations pooled across sixteen courses, 2022–2026: percent of ratings Frequently or Very Frequently, by survey item. Counts are ratings, not students.](/teaching/fig_ta.webp)

The lowest-rated item is feedback on written assignments (85%), concentrated in essay-graded courses where my role was grading. In response, I now annotate each submission in place and add an overall comment on the submission as a whole, so students see both specific fixes and the larger pattern to work on.

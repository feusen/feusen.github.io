**Intermediate Macroeconomics (Econ 102), San José State University, Instructor of Record, Fall 2026.**

## Introduction

The objective of this course is to analyze the aggregate or "macro" behavior of the US economy. The first part of the course examines the economic theory of the long run: the factors that determine income, savings, and consumption, with a special focus on theories of economic growth (the production, Solow, and Romer models). The second part considers short-run business-cycle issues, like how unemployment and inflation change during periods of unusually fast or slow growth, building up the IS curve, monetary policy, and the Phillips curve, and examines current issues in the conduct of monetary and fiscal policy.

## Course Goals

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

+ Analyze the aggregate (macro) behavior of the US economy and understand it in a coherent framework.
+ Explain monetary and fiscal policy responses to short-run and long-run economic problems.
+ Interpret US macro data through the lens of the main macro models.

Textbook: Charles Jones, *Macroeconomics* (4th or 5th edition, W.W. Norton). Prerequisite: Econ 1A.

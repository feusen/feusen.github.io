##### Abstract

In a federal system, is the shared governance of infrastructure assets by sub-national jurisdictions inefficient? We examine this question in the context of highway bridges in the United States, where approximately 800 roadway bridges cross state lines and the responsibility for maintenance is shared between neighboring states. Shared maintenance is associated with a significantly lower sufficiency rating (0.2 SD) and structural condition (0.2 SD) at endline compared with initially similar controls. Consistent with a model of decision-making under shared governance, we find that the treatment effect is largest among bridges that see less traffic. The shared bridge penalty is increasing in proxies for coordination cost and declines in asymmetries between states in bridge value.
